# 04 Presenter guide: ScholarClone client presentation

Keys in the web deck: Right / Space / Enter = next step, Left / Backspace = back, P = presenter notes window, M = five-slide version, F = full screen, C = image credits, Escape = leave a scenario and return to the journey.

## 1. What is your school not seeing?

**Main message.** The school promoter stops and asks what they are missing.

**Say.** Pause on the question. Do not explain anything. Let them answer first: what do you think a school like yours might not be seeing?

**Ask.** What would you say your school is not seeing today?

**Then.** You already hold a lot of information. Let us look at where it comes from.

## 2. What if the information your school already has could help you see more clearly?

**Main message.** The problem is often not missing data; it is data that has not been connected.

**Say.** Every school already produces all of these. Ask which of them the principal sees together on one day.

**Ask.** Which two of these do you look at side by side today?

**Then.** Let us make this real with one student.

## 3. A student keeps struggling. The school has the records. What happens next?

**Main message.** Recording a problem is not the same as knowing the next learning step.

**Say.** Make clear this is an illustration, not a customer story. Ask them to recognise it, not to agree with us.

**Ask.** Does this happen in your school? What usually happens after the marks are in?

**Then.** So what separates a record from a next step?

## 4. Information is not yet action.

**Main message.** Information becomes useful only when it moves through understanding to action and review.

**Say.** Walk the line slowly. Then stop and ask where their information stops. Let the leader pick the chip; follow the leader, not the script.

**Ask.** Where does your school's information stop today? Which situation matters most to you?

**Then.** Let us look at how a school can close that gap.

## 5. An integrated intelligent K–12 platform designed to help schools connect information, support better decisions and take meaningful action.

**Main message.** ScholarClone connects information, supports decisions and helps the school act.

**Say.** Only now introduce the name. Read the definition once in plain words. Tell them the deck will always mark what works today and what is ahead.

**Ask.** Does that match how you would describe what you need?

**Then.** Here is how the pieces fit together.

## 6. Six products, one idea: see, decide, act.

**Main message.** Six products around one idea, each with an honest status.

**Say.** Do not read all six. Say what each one is for in one phrase, then point at the badges: this is where we are honest about status.

**Ask.** Which of these would you start with if you had to choose one?

**Then.** Start with the part that runs the school every day.

## 7. The everyday work of the school, off the registers and into workflows  [LIVE]

**Main message.** The everyday work of the school moves from registers into workflows.

**Say.** Pick the one workflow the leader cares about. Detail for the other four is in the notes and a supporting slide.

**Ask.** Where do you currently find out something has gone wrong in fees or attendance?

**Then.** Operations tell you what happened. Learning shows what to do about it for a student.

## 8. From 'who failed' to 'what to learn next'  [PARTIAL]

**Main message.** From 'which students failed' to 'what each student should learn next'.

**Say.** In plain words: find what the student does not yet understand, plan from it, practise, check, then decide what comes next. Say clearly that students do not set their own goals in the product today.

**Ask.** How does a teacher find out today which concept a student is stuck on?

**Then.** Teachers carry the rest of this load. Here is where AI helps them.

## 9. Less time preparing, more time coaching  [PARTIAL]

**Main message.** AI helps teachers prepare; it does not replace them.

**Say.** Keep it concrete: the teacher picks a chapter and gets drafts to adjust. Do not promise checking or approval steps until verified.

**Ask.** Which part of preparation takes most of your teachers' time today?

**Then.** Teachers also need support to grow. Here is how that can be tracked.

## 10. From occasional training to continuing development  [PARTIAL]

**Main message.** Staff development moves from occasional training to ratings, gaps, a plan and tracked progress.

**Say.** Use an unnamed teacher. Describe ratings, a gap view and a development plan with actions. Be open that a progress view across all staff and automatic learning suggestions are not built for schools yet.

**Ask.** How do you know today whether a training you ran changed what a teacher can do?

**Then.** Everything so far produces signals. Leaders need them in one place.

## 11. See where attention is needed. Decide. Review.  [PARTIAL]

**Main message.** Signals from different functions help a leader see where attention is needed, and a person decides.

**Say.** This is decision support, not automation. Say the comparison shows a link, not a cause. Do not describe it as a forecast or as finding students early.

**Ask.** When a report arrives, who decides what happens next, and where is that recorded?

**Then.** One more thread: where does a student's future fit in?

## 12. A career conversation that starts from evidence  [PARTIAL]

**Main message.** Career conversations can start from a student's interests and learning evidence.

**Say.** Lead with the honest part: when there is not enough evidence the product says so. Never say competencies or any career outcome.

**Ask.** What does career guidance look like for your senior classes today?

**Then.** Now the part that matters most: how these connect.

## 13. What is connected today, and what is not yet  [CONCEPTUAL]

**Main message.** Some connections work today; more are design intent, and we say which is which.

**Say.** Be plain: three connections work today and the rest are where we want to go. That honesty is the trust-builder.

**Ask.** Which missing connection would matter most to your school?

**Then.** Let us put it in a day.

## 14. A principal's morning

**Main message.** A principal's five questions already have answers in different places; one morning view is the next step.

**Say.** Let the principal choose one question and follow it through its product. Say plainly that a single morning view is not built.

**Ask.** Which of these five do you answer first on a normal morning?

**Then.** If this feels worth testing, here is how we would start together.

## 15. Let's discover what your school could see differently.

**Main message.** The next step is a conversation about one useful first use case.

**Say.** Do not sell. Ask which of the four items they can name today and offer to start there.

**Ask.** What is the one challenge you would want to look at first?
